# Interviews 2–5 — Potential patients (21 October 2026)

Format: online, written questions sent in a messenger; answers given in text or voice and
translated into English by the team (edited translation, not verbatim). Participants are
students and young adults aged 18–23; names and contact details are not recorded. The
prototype was not shown in these interviews.

Questions sent (numbering of the interview guide in [02-relevance-evidence.md](../02-relevance-evidence.md)):

| Message | Guide |
|---|---|
| 1. When did you last visit a dentist and why? | Q1 |
| 2. Do you go for check-ups or only when something hurts? | Q2 |
| 3. Would you upload a photo of your teeth to such a website? Why / why not? | Q6 |
| 4. What would make you trust such a service, and what would not? | Q7 |
| 5. Would you be concerned about where your photo is stored? | Q8 |
| 6. The site honestly says "this is not a diagnosis, see a dentist". Is it still useful? | Q12 |

## Interview 2 — Participant P1, 18

1. About a year ago. A parent made the appointment because a tooth hurt; a filling was needed.
2. Does not go while nothing hurts: "I just forget about it".
3. Yes, would try it — if it is just uploading a photo, without registration or a phone number.
4. Would trust it more if dentists took part in the development. If the site immediately starts selling something, would close it.
5. Yes, would not want to see the photo somewhere later; better if it is deleted right away.
6. Yes — it would at least be a reason to make an appointment: "otherwise I put it off until the last moment".

## Interview 3 — Participant P2, 21

1. About three months ago, a professional cleaning; the dentist also checked the teeth.
2. Tries to go at least once a year even if nothing is wrong.
3. Maybe, out of curiosity. But does not understand how to photograph the back teeth properly by oneself.
4. Would want to know who made it and whether dentists checked the results. "We have artificial intelligence" alone is not enough.
5. Yes. Would check whether photos are stored and whether they can be deleted.
6. Yes, but wants an explanation about one's own photo, not the same "see a dentist" for everyone.

## Interview 4 — Participant P3, 20

1. About two years ago; a tooth hurt badly.
2. Only when it hurts: "it is scary, and it is a pity to spend the money".
3. Yes, would upload — it would be calmer to look at home first.
4. Would not believe a site that says "everything is bad" for any photo. A clinic advertisement right away would also look suspicious.
5. Not very concerned if it is only teeth without the name, but the photo must not be shown to other people without permission.
6. Yes. But if the site says nothing was found, "I will most likely postpone the dentist again".

## Interview 5 — Participant P4, 23

1. Half a year ago; an old filling chipped.
2. Intends to go for check-ups, but something always gets in the way, so ends up going with a specific problem.
3. Would try it if it is quick. If an app download, account creation and a questionnaire are required, would probably give up.
4. An example would help: a photo, what the service noticed and what a dentist said. And honesty when a photo is bad and nothing can be seen.
5. Yes, especially if photos are used for training; wants to decide personally whether to allow that.
6. Yes, if it shows which tooth should be checked: "I already know that I should visit a dentist".
