## 1.3 Problem statement

Untreated dental caries in permanent teeth is the most common health condition worldwide
according to the WHO Global Oral Health Status Report (2022). Early lesions are painless and
are usually found only during a dental examination, while many people visit a dentist only
when pain appears. At that point treatment is more complex and expensive.

People have a smartphone camera but no simple tool that tells them whether what they see on
their teeth deserves a visit. Existing AI products in dentistry mostly analyse X-ray images
inside clinics (see [04-existing-systems.md](04-existing-systems.md)) and are not available
to patients. Research models for caries detection on intraoral photographs exist
(see [03-literature-review.md](03-literature-review.md)), but they are rarely delivered as a
usable, measurable software system.

> Verified vs. assumed: the prevalence statement is taken from the WHO report. The claim that
> users lack such a tool and would use it is an **assumption** to be checked by user
> interviews (see [02-relevance-evidence.md](02-relevance-evidence.md)).

## 1.4 Target users

| User | Need |
|---|---|
| Patient (primary) | Check a photo of their teeth at home, understand the result, decide whether to see a dentist, track changes over time |
| Dental clinic | Receive the patient's photo with marked suspicious regions before the visit |
| School prevention programme | Quick mass screening with referral of children to a dentist |

## 1.5 Aim

To develop a web platform that detects visual signs of dental caries on photographs of teeth
using a dedicated neural network inference service, explains the result to the user, and to
experimentally evaluate the platform's detection quality and performance.

## 1.6 Objectives

1. Analyse the literature on caries detection from intraoral photographs and compare
   existing systems and technologies.
2. Collect requirements from potential users and specify functional and non-functional
   requirements with acceptance criteria.
3. Design the architecture: web application, REST API, inference service, storage.
4. Prepare open labelled datasets and train a caries detection model.
5. Implement the backend, the inference service and the web application.
6. Integrate a multimodal LLM to produce plain-language explanations.
7. Evaluate detection quality against a multimodal LLM baseline and measure platform
   performance under load.
8. Deploy the platform and document the results.

## 1.7 Expected outcomes

- A working web platform (deployed, accessible from desktop and mobile browsers).
- A trained caries detection model served by a separate inference service.
- An experimental report: precision, recall, F1 and mAP@0.5 of the trained model compared
  with zero-shot detection by a multimodal LLM; latency and throughput of the platform.
- Documentation: requirements, architecture, test results, explanatory note.

## 1.8 Scope

**In scope**
- User registration and authentication.
- Uploading a photo of teeth (JPEG/PNG) and quality checks (size, blur, whether teeth are
  visible).
- Detection of caries regions with confidence scores; bounding boxes drawn on the photo.
- Plain-language explanation and recommendation generated by a multimodal LLM.
- History of analyses for a user.
- Evaluation pipeline for detection quality and load testing.

**Out of scope**
- Clinical diagnosis and treatment planning. The result is a screening hint only.
- X-ray, panoramic and CBCT images.
- Native mobile applications (the web application is responsive instead).
- Collecting patient photographs: only open datasets with suitable licences are used for
  training and evaluation.

| Dataset | Content | Licence |

## 1.10 Engineering complexity

- Three-tier architecture with a separately deployable inference service and asynchronous
  processing of analysis jobs.
- Training, export (ONNX) and serving of a detection model under latency constraints.
- Integration of an external multimodal LLM with structured output and fallback behaviour.
- Storage of medical images with access control.
- Reproducible evaluation of detection quality and load testing.

## 1.11 Assumptions and constraints

- Open datasets are representative enough of smartphone photos; this is a known risk
  (see project risks in [10-project-management.md](10-project-management.md)).
- The LLM API is paid and rate-limited; experiments must fit the budget.
- Model training is done on a free cloud GPU (Google Colab / Kaggle).
